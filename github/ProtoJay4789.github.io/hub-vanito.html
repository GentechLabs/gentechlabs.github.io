📖 KAGEKŌ — Shadow & Light
The story of two halves becoming one whole
Part I — The Boy Who Screamed at the Moon
He was seven when he learned screaming felt better than crying. His real name died in the alleys of Shinjuku, buried under years of hunger and silence. He grew up invisible — sleeping under pachinko parlors, stealing food, a ghost in the neon machine. Until he found a beat-up black Les Paul in a pawn shop window. He stole it, taught himself in a rented storage unit, turning rage into sound. He called himself KAGE — Shadow — because that's what he was.

On the night everything changed, he played to a crowd of twelve people. Half walked out during his first song. One stayed.

Kono Sora no Shita — Under This Sky · The moment two souls found each other
Part II — The Girl Who Sang to the Stars
Hikari Tanaka grew up in a warm ramen shop in a small coastal town. Her father was her whole world. When he got sick, music became her escape — she'd climb to the roof and sing to the stars, pouring everything she couldn't say into melody. Her father passed when she was sixteen. She packed a bag, got on a train to Tokyo, and found work washing dishes at a live house in Shimokitazawa.

He was at the bar. The quiet guy with dead eyes who'd just screamed his heart out. She walked up, arms crossed: "You're angry. I like it. But you need someone to carry the melody so the screams hit harder." He handed her crumpled lyrics. She read them. "Meet me tomorrow."

Part III — KAGEKŌ
They wrote RISING SUN in four hours in a cramped Koenji studio. A song about two people who shouldn't have found each other — a shadow and a light. They've been inseparable ever since. They fight, they argue, she's thrown a microphone stand at him. But they always find their way back to that studio. Their debut album KAGEKŌ (影光) is the story of two opposites becoming one whole.

Epilogue
He still disappears sometimes. But now she knows where to find him — on the roof, looking at the moon. "The moon used to feel cold when I was a kid," he says. "Like it was watching but didn't care."
"And now?"
He looks at her. A small, crooked smile. "Now it feels warm."
