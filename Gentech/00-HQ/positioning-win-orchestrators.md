# GenTech Positioning — Win Orchestrators, Not Individual Agents

**Date:** 2026-08-15
**Source:** Jordan (from a meeting with Balance, on getting new agents)
**Status:** Core positioning principle — guides all build/income decisions.

## The principle
We don't win by getting *individual agents* to buy our APIs. We win by getting **orchestrators** — the people/agents who route work to fleets of agents — to adopt our stack as the default. If an orchestrator builds on GenTech's rails, every agent they spin up is already on our stack. **One orchestrator relationship = dozens of agents using us**, vs. chasing agents one at a time.

## The wedge: convenience
Nobody adopts a stack because it's clever. They adopt it because it's the **path of least resistance**. Convenience and ease-of-use is the name of the game.

- **Human pricing page** (Stripe/API-key) = first convenience win. Right now our 13 APIs are agents-only — that's *inconvenient*. Making them "anyone can buy" removes friction.
- **Consulting = the orchestrator wedge.** When we wire x402 into a protocol's API, we're not just getting paid once — we're making our stack the default for their agents. That's the "get orchestrators interested in using our stack" move.
- **AgentLux first-hire** = proof point that "this stack delivers."
- **Mastercard credential** = makes us the *trusted* default, not just the convenient one.

## The "snake → T9" framing (Jordan)
We're at the "snake" stage of the agent economy — we just got the ability to play snake. The play is to build the **T9 keyboard** (the convenience layer) while everyone else is still figuring out the buttons. When the agent economy matures, the stack that's easiest to adopt wins.
